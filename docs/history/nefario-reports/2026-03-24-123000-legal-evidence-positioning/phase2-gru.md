## Recommendations

### Claims Matrix: WRL Capabilities to FRE and eIDAS Provisions

The matrix below maps each shipped WRL capability to the specific legal provisions it supports. Claim strength classifications:

- **STRONG** -- the capability directly satisfies the rule's requirements with no additional steps
- **SUPPORTIVE** -- the capability contributes to satisfying the rule but does not alone suffice; additional evidence or procedural steps are required
- **FUTURE** -- requires capabilities not yet shipped or depends on unshipped R41

---

#### FRE 901(b)(9): "Evidence About a Process or System"

**Rule text**: "Evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result."

| WRL Capability | Claim Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Automated headless browser capture | SUPPORTIVE | The capture process is an "electronic process or system" under 901(b)(9). But 901(b)(9) requires *evidence describing* the process AND *showing it produces an accurate result*. WRL's capture pipeline is that process. The documentation, verification checks, and reproducibility support the "produces an accurate result" prong. However, the rule does not self-satisfy -- someone (a qualified person, the system documentation, or a certification) must *describe* the process to the court. WRL provides the raw material for this evidence; it does not itself constitute the evidentiary submission. |
| Ed25519 signatures over bundle hash | SUPPORTIVE | Demonstrates integrity of the captured output. Supports showing "accurate result" by proving the output has not been modified since capture. But signatures alone do not prove the capture process is accurate -- they prove the output is unaltered. |
| SHA-256 individual artifact hashes | SUPPORTIVE | Each file (screenshot, HTML, WARC) is independently hashed and recorded in the manifest. This supports the chain-of-custody argument that the "system" produces verifiable, deterministic results. |
| RFC 3161 timestamps (DigiCert) | SUPPORTIVE | Anchors the capture to a specific time via an independent third party. Strengthens the "accurate result" argument by proving temporal accuracy. Does not alone establish process accuracy. |
| Public verification URL + CLI verifier | SUPPORTIVE | Enables any party (including opposing counsel, the court) to independently verify all integrity claims without trusting WRL. This is powerful supporting evidence for "produces an accurate result" because the verification is independently executable. |
| Key rotation with historical key archive | SUPPORTIVE | Ensures older captures remain verifiable. Supports long-term "accurate result" claims by maintaining the integrity chain across key lifecycles. |
| Open-source verifier (Apache 2.0) | SUPPORTIVE | The verification code itself is auditable. Transparency strengthens the "process" description. |

**Answer to question (a)**: Ed25519 + RFC 3161 does **not** alone satisfy 901(b)(9). The rule requires *evidence describing a process or system* -- it is a framework for authentication, not a checklist that a tool can satisfy in isolation. WRL's cryptographic integrity chain is powerful SUPPORTIVE evidence for a 901(b)(9) authentication argument. The proponent still needs to describe the system to the court (via testimony, declaration, or a 902(13) certification). The correct framing is: "WRL provides the technical foundation for FRE 901(b)(9) authentication -- Ed25519 signatures prove bundle integrity, RFC 3161 timestamps prove temporal accuracy, and independent verification proves the system produces reproducible, verifiable results." Do NOT claim "satisfies FRE 901(b)(9)."

#### FRE 902(13): "Certified Records Generated by an Electronic Process or System"

**Rule text**: "A record generated by an electronic process or system that produces an accurate result, as shown by a certification of a qualified person that complies with the certification requirements of Rule 902(11) or (12)."

| WRL Capability | Claim Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Automated capture process | FUTURE | WRL captures are "records generated by an electronic process." This is the correct rule. But 902(13) requires a *certification from a qualified person* -- specifically, someone who can describe the process and attest that it produces accurate results. R41 (certification document) is not shipped. Without the certification document, 902(13) cannot be invoked for self-authentication. |
| SHA-256 hashes + Ed25519 signature | FUTURE | These provide the cryptographic evidence that would be *referenced in* a 902(13) certification. But without the certification document itself, they support 901(b)(9) (with testimony) rather than enabling 902(13) self-authentication. |
| WACZ bundle with full artifact manifest | FUTURE | The deterministic bundle structure would be described in the certification. The bundle is the "record" that 902(13) would authenticate. Ready as the underlying evidence, but needs the certification wrapper. |

**Answer to question (c)**: 902(13) **cannot be claimed as a shipped capability**. It must be entirely deferred to post-R41. However, the landing page CAN reference 902(13) as a *designed-for* target, provided it is crystal clear that the certification document is a future capability. Acceptable framing: "Designed for FRE 902(13) self-authentication (certification document coming soon)." Unacceptable framing: "Supports FRE 902(13)" without qualification -- this implies the capability exists today. The safest approach is to omit 902(13) entirely until R41 ships, and then add it as a concrete claim.

#### FRE 902(14): "Certified Data Copied from an Electronic Device, Storage Medium, or File"

**Rule text**: "Data copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file, if authenticated by a process of digital identification as shown by a certification of a qualified person."

| WRL Capability | Claim Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| SHA-256 hashes per artifact | STRONG | Hash comparison is the canonical "process of digital identification" that 902(14) contemplates. The Advisory Committee Notes explicitly cite hash value comparison as the exemplary method. WRL computes and records SHA-256 hashes for every artifact in the WACZ bundle. Any party can recompute the hashes and compare them to the recorded values. |
| WACZ bundle integrity verification | STRONG | The entire verification chain (individual hashes -> manifest hash -> signature -> timestamp) is exactly the kind of "digital identification process" that 902(14) was designed for. |
| CLI and REST verification | SUPPORTIVE | Enables the "qualified person" to demonstrate the digital identification process without proprietary tools. Strengthens the certification but is not itself the certification. |

**Important caveat**: 902(14) still requires a *certification* from a qualified person. WRL provides the technical infrastructure that makes the certification straightforward (the hashes, the verification tools, the open documentation), but the proponent's qualified person must still write the actual certification. This is procedural, not a product feature -- but it means WRL cannot claim "self-authenticating under 902(14)" as a turnkey capability. The correct framing: "WRL's SHA-256 hash chain provides the digital identification process contemplated by FRE 902(14)."

**Answer to question (b)**: 902(14) is actually the more natural FRE provision for WRL than 902(13), despite the issue spec's emphasis on 902(13). Here is why: 902(14) is about authenticating *copies* via digital identification (hash comparison). WRL captures are, in a sense, certified copies of web content at a point in time, with hash-based integrity verification. The "qualified person" certification under 902(14) is simpler than 902(13) -- it only needs to attest that the hashing process works, not describe the entire capture system.

Regarding the question about 902(14)'s "certified records of a regularly conducted activity" -- that is actually **Rule 803(6) / 902(11)**, not 902(14). The question conflates two rules. 902(14) is about digital copies authenticated by hash comparison, which is directly applicable. The business records exception (803(6), authenticated via 902(11)) would apply to the *capturing organization's* use of WRL (i.e., a company that routinely uses WRL to archive its web properties could authenticate those records as business records of its regularly conducted archival activity). WRL itself is not "keeping records of its own activities" in the 803(6) sense -- it is a tool that *others* use to create records.

#### eIDAS Article 41: "Legal effects of electronic time stamps"

**Article 41(1)**: "An electronic time stamp shall not be denied legal effect and admissibility as evidence in legal proceedings solely on the grounds that it is in an electronic form or that it does not meet the requirements of the qualified electronic time stamp."

**Article 41(2)**: "A qualified electronic time stamp shall enjoy the presumption of the accuracy of the date and the time it indicates and the integrity of the data to which the date and time are bound."

**Article 41(3)**: "A qualified electronic time stamp issued in one Member State shall be recognised as a qualified electronic time stamp in all Member States."

| WRL Capability | Claim Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Standard RFC 3161 timestamp (DigiCert) | STRONG for Art. 41(1) | Article 41(1) establishes non-discrimination: even non-qualified electronic timestamps cannot be denied legal effect solely because they are electronic. Every WRL capture with a standard timestamp benefits from this baseline protection in EU proceedings. |
| eIDAS-qualified RFC 3161 timestamp (Sectigo, account opt-in) | STRONG for Art. 41(2) | When a tenant has eIDAS qualified timestamps enabled, their captures receive a timestamp from a qualified TSA. This directly triggers Article 41(2)'s legal presumption: the date, time, and integrity of the bound data are presumed accurate. This is a rebuttable presumption, meaning the burden shifts to the challenging party. |
| eIDAS-qualified timestamp + cross-border recognition | STRONG for Art. 41(3) | Qualified timestamps are automatically recognized across all EU Member States. No additional certification needed per jurisdiction. |
| Dual-TSA assembly (standard + qualified) | SUPPORTIVE | The belt-and-suspenders approach means a capture has independent timestamp evidence from two separate authorities. This strengthens the overall temporal claim but does not add a distinct legal provision. |

**Answer to question (d)**: Yes, "presumption of accuracy of the date and time it indicates and the integrity of the data to which the date and time are bound" is the correct and precise framing from Article 41(2). The key qualifier is that this is a *rebuttable* presumption -- it shifts the burden of proof to anyone challenging the timestamp, but it does not make the timestamp conclusive. WRL should use this exact phrasing (or close to it) rather than vaguer formulations like "legally binding" or "guaranteed accurate."

Correct claim: "Qualified timestamps enjoy a legal presumption of accuracy under eIDAS Article 41(2) across all EU Member States."

Incorrect claim: "Qualified timestamps are legally binding proof of time."

### Recommended Legal Framing Language

For the updated landing page and docs, use this graduated precision:

**Strongest claims (can use without qualification):**
- "Independently verifiable web captures"
- "Ed25519 digital signatures with RFC 3161 timestamps from independent authorities"
- "SHA-256 hash integrity for every captured artifact"
- "eIDAS-qualified timestamps with Article 41(2) presumption of accuracy" (for accounts with eIDAS enabled)
- "Open verification -- anyone can confirm integrity without trusting the capture service"

**Supportive claims (accurate with context):**
- "Supports FRE 901(b)(9) authentication of electronic processes" -- accurate because the captures provide the technical evidence a proponent would use
- "SHA-256 hash chain provides the digital identification process contemplated by FRE 902(14)" -- accurate, specific, and grounded in the Advisory Committee Notes
- "Designed for use as evidence in legal proceedings" -- not claiming admissibility, claiming design intent

**Claims to defer (until R41 ships):**
- Any reference to FRE 902(13) self-authentication
- "Self-authenticating" as a product claim
- "No expert testimony required" (this is the 902(13) promise, which requires the certification document)

**Claims to avoid entirely:**
- "Legally admissible" -- this is a court determination
- "Court-ready evidence" -- implies guaranteed acceptance
- "FRCP compliant" -- vague and technically about civil procedure, not evidence rules
- "Meets legal requirements" -- which requirements, in which jurisdiction?
- "Certified" or "notarized" -- WRL does not provide legal certification or notarization

## Risks and Concerns

### 1. The 902(13) Gap is Real and Visible

The R42 issue spec lists "FRE 902(13) - self-authenticating certification documents" as a success criterion. R41 is not shipped. If the landing page references 902(13) without the certification document, it is overclaiming. Recommendation: omit 902(13) entirely from R42 scope and treat it as a follow-up after R41 ships. The R42 success criteria should be amended to reflect this dependency.

### 2. eIDAS Production Readiness

Phase 0063 outcome notes several deferred items: no end-to-end test against the real qualified TSA, Sectigo endpoint URL not verified, QUALIFIED_TSA_AUTH secret not provisioned for production. If eIDAS is not actually working in production, claiming "eIDAS-qualified timestamps" on the landing page is overclaiming. **Verify production deployment status before adding eIDAS claims.**

### 3. "Proof" vs. "Evidence" vs. "Verification" -- Terminology Matters

In cryptography, "proof" means mathematical certainty. In law, "proof" means persuasion of a trier of fact. In marketing, "proof" means trust signal. These are different things. The landing page currently uses "proof" loosely. The legal evidence positioning effort must be disciplined about which sense is intended. Default to "verification" (cryptographic accuracy) and "evidence" (material supporting a legal argument) rather than "proof" (which implies conclusiveness).

### 4. Jurisdiction Scope

FRE applies to US federal courts. Many US state courts have adopted equivalent rules but not all. eIDAS applies to EU Member States. The landing page should not imply global legal standing. Recommended qualifier: "in US federal proceedings (FRE) and EU proceedings (eIDAS)."

### 5. Page Vault / Competitor Positioning Precedent

Competitors like Page Vault are careful to position as "supporting authentication" rather than "guaranteeing admissibility." They include prominent disclaimers and frame FRE references as educational, not as product claims. WRL should follow this same pattern -- describe the legal framework, explain how WRL's capabilities map to it, and include a disclaimer. This is not timidity; it is the industry standard and legally prudent.
