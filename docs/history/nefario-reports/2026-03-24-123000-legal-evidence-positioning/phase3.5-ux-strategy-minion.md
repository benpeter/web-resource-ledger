## Verdict: APPROVE

The plan is well-structured from a UX perspective. Approve with two targeted notes for the implementation agents.

### What works

**Journey coherence is strong.** Landing page card -> docs guide -> verification page forms a correct progressive disclosure chain. Each layer serves a distinct audience job without duplicating the layer below. Legal professionals can enter from search directly at the docs guide and orient themselves from the opening paragraph. The cross-referencing strategy (link to verification.md, don't duplicate it) is correct.

**Cognitive load decisions are sound.** Keeping FRE rule numbers out of the hero and inside the self-selection card is the right call. Hero audience-neutrality is preserved. The legal card is more complex than the other three, but the mitigating decision to add "Learn more" links to all four cards restores visual parity. Single-page-with-anchors for the docs guide is correct for an audience that reads to build a complete argument.

**Disclaimer placement is correct.** Bottom-of-page is standard practice for informational legal disclaimers. A top-of-page disclaimer would signal defensiveness before the reader has context to interpret it — this was decided correctly.

**Competitor comparison without brand names is correct.** Descriptive patterns age better, avoid backlash, and feel like documentation rather than a sales tool.

### Notes for implementation agents

**1. Two comparison tables in sequence (docs guide Sections 5 and 6)**

The guide contains two comparison tables: one contrasting WRL against traditional preservation approaches (Section 5), and one comparing verification approaches across capture service patterns (Section 6). These serve different reader needs but appear back-to-back. Without a clear transitional sentence, readers may not understand why two comparison tables exist.

The implementing agent should add a brief orienting sentence before Section 6 that distinguishes what it adds beyond Section 5. Something like: "The preceding comparison covers preservation approaches broadly. The following focuses specifically on how verification independence varies across dedicated capture services."

This is a minor authoring concern, not a structural issue.

**2. "Planned" for FRE 902(13) must be visually unambiguous**

The target audience is legal professionals who will read carefully. The plan specifies that 902(13) should be "clearly marked as Planned" but leaves implementation to the agent. Legal readers skim headings before reading paragraphs. If "Planned" appears only in paragraph text, it will be missed by some readers.

Requirement for Task 2: the 902(13) section heading must include the "Planned" marker, not just the paragraph body. Acceptable forms: a heading badge, a parenthetical in the heading ("Rule 902(13): Self-Authenticating Certification (Planned)"), or a prominent callout at the top of the section. Do not rely on paragraph-level placement alone.
